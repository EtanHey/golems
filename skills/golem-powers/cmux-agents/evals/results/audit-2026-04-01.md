## Methodology

The historical audit compared a no-skill baseline with a run that loaded the
full `cmux-agents` skill. It evaluated spawning, communication, monitoring,
lifecycle, and workflow behavior. The retained findings are qualitative only.

## Strongest qualitative discriminators

- Worker/audit pane layout rules were ecosystem-specific.
- The biometric delay and agent-registry format required local knowledge.
- BrainLayer write staggering and retrospective storage were encoded workflow rules.
- The collab template path and required sections were not discoverable from tool schemas.

## Weakest qualitative discriminators

- Listing surfaces and agents was partly discoverable from the MCP schema.
- Paired input/key delivery and completion notification followed common tool-use patterns.
- Generic lifecycle inspection was less diagnostic than ecosystem-specific recovery behavior.

## Issues found

- Registry placement was difficult to enforce from response structure alone.
- Pane-direction instructions did not specify every split parameter.
- The BrainLayer progress-store step was easy to miss during recovery.

## Changes from the previous audit

- Redundant wait cases were consolidated.
- A nonexistent screen-reading parameter case was replaced with registry behavior.
- Keyword matching was replaced with behavioral assertions.
- Coverage was added for biometric delay, delivery verification, registry maintenance,
  monitoring after spawn, BrainLayer staggering, retrospectives, and collab templates.

## Historical verdict

The audit found useful qualitative discriminators, but its original numeric verdict
is not publishable without provenance-complete reruns.
